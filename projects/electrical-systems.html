1.0 Overview

Two linked projects: characterizing RC and RL circuits, then designing and validating filters built from the same components. Every experimental measurement was checked against both hand calculation and SPICE simulation.

2.0 RC and RL characterization

RC circuit

With R = 100 Ω and C = 0.1 µF, the theoretical time constant is:

τ = RC = 100 × 0.1×10⁻⁶ = 10 µs

The turn-on transient across the resistor follows VR(t) = Vs·e−t/τ, so at t = τ, VR = 0.368 Vs.

Measured: τ = 9.573 µs against 10 µs theoretical — a 4.3% difference attributable to component tolerance and measurement uncertainty.

RL circuit

With R = 100 Ω and L = 1 mH:

τ = L/R = 0.001 / 100 = 10 µs

Here the transient follows VR(t) = Vs·(1 − e−t/τ), giving 0.632 Vs at t = τ.

Measured: τ = 8.969 µs. The larger gap is consistent with inductor winding resistance, which the ideal model omits.

3.0 Filter design

RC low-pass and high-pass

fc = 1 / (2πRC) = 15.92 kHz

The low-pass passes signals below 15.92 kHz and attenuates above; the high-pass, built from the same component values, gives the complementary response with the transition at the same cutoff. Transfer curves were measured on a network analyzer and compared against LTspice.

RLC bandpass

With R = 100 Ω, L = 1 mH, C = 0.1 µF:

fR = 1 / (2π√(LC)) = 15,915 Hz  ·  B = 100 kHz  ·  Q = 0.159

The low Q is worth noting: with a 100 Ω series resistance the bandwidth far exceeds the resonant frequency, so this circuit is a broad bandpass rather than a selective one.

4.0 Simulation and validation

All filters were simulated in LTspice, and the simulated transfer curves matched the bench measurements:

  • Magnitude and phase curves agreed between simulation and experiment
  • Cutoff frequencies aligned within measurement tolerance
  • Roll-off and resonance behavior were predicted accurately
